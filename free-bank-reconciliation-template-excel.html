Sheet 1: Statement check

Most reconciliation problems start before the reconciliation: a transaction lost while copying from a PDF, a 6 read as an 8, a row pasted twice. This sheet catches those.

  1. Delete the example rows and paste your transactions from row 6: date, description, amount, and the balance printed next to each transaction.
  2. Amounts go with a sign: money in positive, money out negative. If your statement has separate debit and credit columns, put =credit-debit in the amount column.
  3. Type the opening balance in B3 and the closing balance in B4.

Column E keeps a running balance and column F compares it with the printed one. Every row should say OK. The first row that says CHECK is where the problem is: that amount was misread, or a transaction is missing or duplicated just above it. The rows after it also turn red because the error carries forward; fix the first one and they go back to OK. Cell E3 must show 0, which proves the total of your amounts matches the gap between the opening and closing balances.

Sheet 2: Bank reconciliation

The classic two-sided reconciliation. The bank balance comes from sheet 1 automatically. You fill in:

  • Deposits in transit: money you recorded that the bank hasn't shown yet.
  • Outstanding cheques or payments: payments you recorded that haven't cleared.
  • Your book balance, plus interest the bank added and fees it charged that aren't in your books yet.

The difference at the bottom turns green at 0. If it doesn't, the gap is usually one of three things: a transaction recorded with the wrong amount on one side, a bank fee nobody booked, or a payment recorded in the wrong month. For a method to find which, see how to reconcile a bank statement in Excel, which matches the two lists with COUNTIFS.

Why check against the printed balance?

Totals can hide errors. If one row is misread by +9 and another by -9, the total still matches. The running balance on each line doesn't let that through: the first wrong row breaks the chain. Banks print that balance on almost every statement, so it's a free audit of your data entry.
