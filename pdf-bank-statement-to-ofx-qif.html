PDF bank statement to OFX or QIF: which format your money app needs

Finance apps import OFX and QIF files with less fuss than CSV, because those formats spell out which value is the date, the amount and the payee. If all you have is a PDF statement, you need to convert it first. Here's how to pick the format.

OFX vs QIF

OFX (Open Financial Exchange; QFX is Quicken's branded variant) is the newer format. Each line carries a transaction ID, which apps use to skip lines they've already imported from the same source. QIF is an older plain-text format that almost every app still reads. It has no transaction IDs, so avoiding duplicates is up to you, and its dates can be read as either month-first or day-first.

Getting from PDF to OFX or QIF

  1. Ask the bank first. Many banks offer OFX, QFX or QIF downloads for recent months. That file is exact, because nothing is read off a PDF.
  2. For older periods or closed accounts, extract the transactions from the PDF into a spreadsheet (our PDF-to-Excel guide covers three free ways), then check them against the statement's balances.
  3. Write the file. QIF is simple enough to build from a spreadsheet: a !Type:Bank line, then for each transaction a D date line, a T amount line, a P payee line and a ^ separator. OFX has a stricter structure, so a converter saves time.
!Type:Bank
D01/03/2026
T-6.45
PSTARBUCKS #2231
^
D01/04/2026
T1312.30
PCLIENT PAYMENT - ACME LLC
^

Check before you import

Undoing a bad import is tedious in most money apps. Before importing, confirm that the opening balance plus every amount equals the closing balance printed on the statement. If it's off by even a cent, a row was dropped or misread.
