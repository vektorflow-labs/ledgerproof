How to reconcile a bank statement in Excel

A bank reconciliation answers one question: does the cash in your books match the cash the bank says you have, and if it doesn't, which items explain the gap? You don't need accounting software for it. Two lists, a few formulas and one final check are enough.

What you need

  • The bank side: every transaction on the statement for the period, with date, description and a signed amount (money in positive, money out negative), plus the opening and closing balance printed on the statement.
  • The book side: the same period from your cash book, ledger or accounting export, also with signed amounts, plus your book balance at the end of the period.

Put each list on its own sheet, called Bank and Books, with the same columns: A = Date, B = Description, C = Amount. If the bank side is still a PDF, convert it first (see PDF bank statement to Excel).

Step 1: make sure the bank list is complete

Everything below assumes the bank sheet holds every row on the statement. If a row got lost when you copied or converted it, you'll spend an hour hunting a "difference" that's really a missing line. Check it first:

= opening_balance + SUM(Bank!C:C) - closing_balance     → should be 0.00

If the statement prints a balance after each line, you can find the exact row that breaks: with balances in column D, put =ROUND(D1+C2-D2,2) in E2 and fill down. Any cell that isn't 0 is where a row was dropped, doubled or misread.

Step 2: match the two lists

The simplest matching key is the amount. On the Bank sheet, in D2:

=COUNTIFS(Books!C:C, C2) >= COUNTIFS(C$2:C2, C2)

This returns TRUE when the books contain at least as many entries of that amount as you've seen so far on the bank side, so two identical $50.00 payments need two $50.00 entries in the books. Put the mirror formula on the Books sheet:

=COUNTIFS(Bank!C:C, C2) >= COUNTIFS(C$2:C2, C2)

Filter both sheets on FALSE. What's left is the reconciliation work. If you have many repeated amounts (rent, subscriptions), match on amount and date instead by adding a helper column like =C2&"|"&TEXT(A2,"yyyy-mm") and counting that.

Step 3: sort the leftovers

Each unmatched line falls into one of a few buckets:

  • In the books, not yet on the statement: checks you wrote that haven't cleared (outstanding checks) and deposits made at the end of the period (deposits in transit). These are timing differences and need no correction.
  • On the statement, not in the books: bank fees, interest, card charges, direct debits nobody recorded. These need a journal entry in your books.
  • In both, but different amounts: usually a typo, like 1,280 entered as 1,230. Fix the books.
  • In both, but entered twice on one side: duplicates. Delete the extra entry.

Step 4: prove it

Lay out the classic two-column reconciliation on a third sheet:

Bank closing balance
  + deposits in transit
  - outstanding checks
  = adjusted bank balance

Book balance
  + interest and credits not yet recorded
  - fees and debits not yet recorded
  +/- error corrections
  = adjusted book balance

adjusted bank balance - adjusted book balance  → should be 0.00

Use SUMIFS on a "Bucket" column you add to the leftovers, so the totals update when you reclassify a line. Wrap the final difference in ROUND(...,2): floating point can leave a 0.0000001 that looks like a real difference.

When it still doesn't balance

  • Difference divisible by 9: often two digits swapped (54.10 entered as 45.10).
  • Difference equal to twice an amount: an item recorded with the wrong sign.
  • Difference equal to an amount on the statement: a row missing from one side. Re-run the Step 1 check.
  • Last month's reconciliation: if it didn't balance either, the gap may have been carried forward. Start from the last period that did.
